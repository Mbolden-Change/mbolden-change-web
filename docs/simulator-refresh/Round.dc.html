SNAP Benefits Reduction (OBBB)

−$202/month

H.R. 1, popularly known as the One Big Beautiful Bill (OBBB), cuts $186 billion from SNAP over 10 years — one of the largest cuts in SNAP history. Work requirements are expanded to ages 55–64. Internet costs are stripped from shelter deductions, reducing benefits by ~$10/month for 65% of households. Starting FY2028, states must cost-share food benefits for the first time ever. (Note: This round compresses the projected 10-year impact into a single period for simulation purposes. Some provisions, including state cost-sharing, phase in through FY2028.)

SNAP (Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program), commonly known as food stamps, is the nation's largest anti-hunger program. It provides monthly funds on an EBT card that families use to buy groceries. Eligibility is based on income and household size. For many families, SNAP is the difference between eating and going hungry.
Nationally

SNAP serves 42 million Americans. CBO estimates 4.7 million people will lose average monthly enrollment. Up to 1.4 million children are at risk of losing automatic certification for free school meals (Urban Institute).
